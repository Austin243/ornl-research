MLIP benchmark

Pretrained interatomic potentials along reaction paths

Pretrained MLIPs are increasingly used to explore reactions. An accurate description of the reactant and product, however, does not ensure an accurate barrier, which also depends on the transition state. We therefore compared fifteen checkpoints with DFT forces, energies and barriers along the 9,017 gas-phase organic reactions of DORTS-9K, from the reactant through the transition state to the product.

Findings

Errors along the reaction path

Checkpoints trained on molecular data reproduce the reference most closely. UMA-M with its OMol head has a median force error of 0.018 eV/Å over all configurations and a barrier error of 0.77 kcal/mol on the 3,995 CHNO reactions, with 96% of the barriers within 1 kcal/mol. The task head matters more than the size of the model. When UMA-M uses its OMC head instead, its median force error rises from 0.018 to 0.49 eV/Å.

The force error grows toward the transition state, where it is two to three times larger than at the reactant and product. The largest barrier errors are also shared among models. All 38 reactions with the largest UMA-M OMol errors are underestimated, by 25–34 kcal/mol, and most are hydrogen transfers involving nitrous acid or reactions from one azide–isocyanate template. Fifteen of the seventeen checkpoints underestimate every one of these barriers, and only the two AIMNet2 checkpoints avoid this failure.

Cost

Accuracy and computational cost

The most accurate checkpoint is also the slowest, at about 28 ms per configuration. UMA-S OMol is four times faster with a median force error of 0.026 eV/Å, and OrbMol-v2 needs about 1 ms per configuration at 0.036 eV/Å.

Method

Each reaction contributes five direct anchors, namely the reactant, the product, the GFN2-xTB transition state and two maximum-slope points, together with normal-mode samples around them. Every configuration carries a ωB97M-V/def2-TZVP single point. We evaluated each checkpoint on all configurations and compared forces, per-atom relative energies, barriers by reaction class and by position along the path, and GPU throughput.

This site is an experimental living project, written in the spirit of a living manuscript. It presents ongoing work and manuscripts in preparation and is updated periodically, so sections will be added and revised as the research develops. Building and maintaining a coded archive of this kind once took considerable effort, but recent AI tools have reduced that effort substantially. Results can therefore be shared with the team, and possibly with other interested groups, in a clean and interactive form while the research is still under way.
